# WRAP Techniques Reference (English)

## W — Widen Your Options

### Multitracking
Do not eliminate options one by one. Pursue 2–3 directions in parallel. Ask: "If you didn't have to choose just one, which two paths could you advance simultaneously?"

### Laddering
1. **Local bright spots**: Successes already inside your team or organization?
2. **Regional best practices**: Who does this better in your industry or city?
3. **Distant analogies**: How do other fields solve structurally similar problems?

### Find someone who solved your problem
Ask: "Who has already faced this decision? What did they choose? How did it turn out?" Prefer reachable people over abstract research.

### Vanishing options test
"If none of your current options were available, what would you do?" Forces escape from narrow framing.

### Baseline options
| Option | Signal |
|--------|--------|
| Do nothing (status quo) | Change cost > expected benefit |
| Delay | Insufficient info; waiting is cheap |
| Combine | Take strengths from multiple options |
| Small experiment (ooch) | Reversible; fast feedback |

## R — Reality-Test Your Assumptions

### Disconfirming questions
- "If this option is wrong, what's the earliest signal?"
- "What's the strongest argument against this decision?"
- "Who loses from my choice? What's their perspective?"

### Zoom out (base rates / outside view)
Ask: "For people making similar decisions, what's the typical outcome distribution?" Avoid survivor-bias anecdotes.

### Zoom in (texture / inside view)
Ask: "What unique details in this specific case change the general pattern?"

### Ooch (small experiment)
Replace prediction with low-cost action:
- Take a small project before a full job switch
- Sell 10 units before building supply chain
- Interview 5 target users before writing a full business plan

### Devil's advocate
Assign someone (or yourself) to argue the opposite case before committing.

## A — Attain Distance Before Deciding

### 10/10/10
- **10 minutes**: What emotion dominates right now?
- **10 months**: How will this decision affect daily life?
- **10 years**: Looking back, what matters most?

### Friend test
"If your best friend were in exactly this situation, what would you advise?"

### Successor test
"If you were replaced tomorrow, what would your successor do about this pending decision?"

### Core priorities
When the decision is agonizing, ask: "Among all goals, which is absolutely non-negotiable?" Then go on the **offensive** to protect it.

---

## P — Prepare to Be Wrong

### Premortem
1. Assume the decision was implemented and **failed completely** in 6–12 months
2. Each person writes 3 likely failure causes
3. Aggregate → identify warning signals → convert to tripwires or mitigations

### Preparade
Assume **unexpected success**:
- What breaks first (capacity, quality, relationships)?
- What resources or processes must be prepared in advance?

### Bookend the future
Future is a **range**, not a point. Map best-case and worst-case; prepare for both.

### Tripwire
Observable, measurable signals that trigger re-evaluation:

| Type | Example |
|------|---------|
| Time-based | Re-evaluate if X hasn't happened in 90 days |
| Metric-based | Stop if conversion < 2% for 4 consecutive weeks |
| Event-based | Activate Plan B if key partner exits |
| Partition-based | Re-evaluate if job satisfaction < 6/10 in first 30 days |

References: David Lee Roth's brown M&M (small signal detecting big problems); Zappos' $1,000 quit offer (culture-fit tripwire).

## Villain ↔ Step Quick Reference

```
Narrow framing      → W (Widen Your Options)
Confirmation bias   → R (Reality-Test Your Assumptions)
Short-term emotion  → A (Attain Distance Before Deciding)
Overconfidence      → P (Prepare to Be Wrong)
```

If multiple villains appear, still walk W→R→A→P once, marking each step as applicable or skipped.
